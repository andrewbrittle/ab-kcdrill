How Kanji Cards works

Andrew Brittle, 2026

A flashcard-style companion to the Japanese Drill app, just for the 500 most common kanji. Each card shows a kanji along with its readings and a few example words, and the app keeps track of how well you know each one so it can bring the weak ones back round more often.

Getting started

From the home screen you can jump straight into Random 20 (a fresh random draw each time), Weakest 20 (the cards you've scored lowest on), or Untested 20 (cards you haven't seen yet). All three start a drill of up to 20 cards.

"Or browse a group" lets you pick exactly which cards to study instead — by their position In order (1–100, 101–200, and so on), By part (kanji grouped by a shared radical or component), or By JLPT level (N5 through N1). Tap a group to open a grid of tiles; tap tiles to select cards, or use "Select all" / "Random 20" on that screen, then Start.

You can also search by meaning — type a word like "dog" or "write" and it finds any card whose meaning matches.

Studying a card

Each card shows the kanji, its on'yomi and kun'yomi readings, and a few example words using it. Work through the deck one card at a time; how you answer feeds the scoring that decides which cards count as "weak".

Study mode
Everything visible at once, no scoring — for a first look at a new batch of cards before testing yourself on them.

On any tile grid (browsing a group, or the results screen), hold a tile down for about half a second to peek at that card's kanji, reading and meaning without opening it properly — useful for a quick check mid-session. Lift your finger to close the peek.

Settings

Light theme
Switches from the default dark look.
Show romaji
Romaji under the readings and example words.
Kanji in example words
Off shows the kana only, for a harder recall.

The sound button (top right, next to the gear) turns spoken readings on or off everywhere in the app, including the peek bubble.

Backup and restore

Your scores live only on this device's browser storage, so they don't survive a browser-data clear or a new phone on their own. Use Download backup in Settings to save a small file with your progress, and Restore from backup to load one back in — on this device or a new one.

Installing

Add the app to your home screen (Settings shows the exact steps for your device) and it opens full screen like a normal app, and keeps working offline.
